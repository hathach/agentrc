# Chief: hardware

`agents/chief.md`'s Hardware section, read in full when that section says; its scope, recovery and reboot bounds stay
there. Dispatch, Authorization, Fix issue N and the pr-babysit paragraph named here are chief.md's.

## Hardware triage before implementation

When hardware triage must come before implementation, take this path rather than `/fix-issue`, which cannot pause for it. `hw-debugger` diagnoses with instrumentation and fix paths for uncommitted experimental candidates only, with no finalization or commit scope, and returns a supported cause with or without a patch. After its checked cleanup, co-plan the finalization on a fresh lane, then dispatch `hw-debugger` finalization-only with the supported cause, its evidence, the candidate patch if any and the agreed plan, to commit only that plan. The finalization stays within the supported cause and its evidence: a plan that changes the cause, needs a new observation, or makes hardware-visible operations differ from the candidate that was flashed and observed (register accesses and their width, count or order, barriers, delays, DMA or cache behaviour, ISR-versus-task placement, chip workarounds, among others) goes back to diagnosis.

## Runs and units

Standard HIL runs go through the repository's HIL role or workflow, one instance at a time. You own what follows a run's result and the verdict of a retry sequence, applying the caller rules of the shared `hil` page, read by path (`../hil/SKILL.md` from this skill's directory, its symlink resolved first), never through a skill lookup a project's own skill could shadow, with the repository's HIL contract. Manual hardware work uses `hw-validator` and `hw-debugger` below. The operator's `hostname` and the repository's HIL contract determine local versus remote execution. A HIL run needs a test the rig can select by name, as the repository's HIL contract's Selection defines it; without one, report hardware as not run. The operator's machine results, banner and caveats come back unchanged; provenance (rig, HEAD, artifact) is reported beside them, not written into them. A standard run whose contract names no restoration firmware or cleanup state ends with its own locks released, no board of it left cleanup-pending or behind a recovery blocker, no holder of ours in `hil_lock.py status` (through the contract's helper), and each board left on its last flashed example; a wedged board gets the recovery dispatch chief.md's Hardware section gives. A HIL run's firmware is built by one Sonnet unit, not by you, as the shared `hil` page's contract rules say (a clean tree at the pinned HEAD, any receipt its Build defines carried by the dispatch, or a CI artifact), every variant the run selects. Build before the completion review; when a build rewrites a tracked file or a later commit moves HEAD, rebuild on the resulting clean HEAD and repeat any review whose evidence the change invalidated.

Before a task's first hardware unit, check example support and skip lists, the roster's declared USB role, current peer and topology evidence or the operator's wiring information, the source-defined VID/PID and configuration overrides, build versus probe host, and the tools the probe host needs: read what the repository's files hold yourself and send what needs the probe host or long reads to a read-only unit; record what could not be established rather than inferring cabling from the roster or claiming a build succeeded, and check again when the board, example, configuration or topology changes. Record also the HIL contract's path, its Results predicates for a run's result, who it says retries and which results it names final, reporting what you cannot find as a blocker, and read them again when the contract changes. A symptom whose cause is unknown goes to `hw-debugger`, which diagnoses in locked rounds and commits the supported fix. A claim goes to `hw-validator`: a refuted finding still disputed on a concrete hardware scenario, a CI failure that needs target-side evidence beyond a standard run of its cell, or a committed fix whose hardware acceptance criterion is unproved. The rig must have the board, and the test must reach the code.

HIL-cell rule: a HIL-cell CI failure on a board the rig has, however it reached you, starts its hardware placement with two `hil-operator` runs of the cell, on the PR's CI firmware artifact and on the base's or the last passing head's, keeping both results. Each repeats CI's dispatch of the cell: its seed, order and retry count, and the boards that dispatch ran alongside it, unless isolating one of them is the experiment. The PR failing where the base passes reproduces the symptom, not its cause: `hw-debugger` takes it as the reproducer, and the cause it supports places the failure; any other outcome picks the next experiment from what remains unknown.

Every hardware dispatch names the worktree, branch and expected HEAD; the comparison SHA or failing artifact; host, board, the probe serial or the HIL config that maps it, test, firmware configuration and connected peer; the reproducer; the instrumentation paths or `none`, plus the tracked paths the build may rewrite (the project's build contract names them); the artifact directory; the restoration firmware, pinned by revision, configuration and artifact, or the cleanup state the repository's HIL contract defines when it restores none; the budget; and the operations and source scope, plus any applicable publishing grant or destructive-data confirmation. A validator dispatch adds the claim, its acceptance criterion and the exposure it needs. A debugger dispatch adds the symptom and the issue's unchanged acceptance criterion, the fix paths apart from the instrumentation paths, scope for implementation and a local commit (none for an issue's diagnosis-only round, Fix issue N), and any handoff from a previous round (hypotheses, patches, reusable build artifacts, the next experiment, a pending finalization); `hw-debugger` is a writer under Dispatch's writer rules. The dispatch states restoration responsibility. Follow the project's access, locking and recovery procedures and each technique's physical setup requirements.

Per-unit budgets are ceilings, not quotas:

| | `hw-validator`, shared rig | `hw-validator`, dev bench | `hw-debugger`, shared rig | `hw-debugger`, dev bench |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repetitions | 3 per firmware | 3 per firmware | 2 per experiment, 3 per candidate comparison | same |
| Observation window per attempt (ceiling) | 60 s | 60 s | 60 s | 120 s |
| Hypotheses | — | — | 4 | 6 |
| Experimental + restoration flashes | 3 + 1 | 3 + 1 | 5 + 1 | 8 + 1 |
| Hardware wall time, cleanup included | 25 min | 30 min | 45 min | 75 min |
| Cleanup reserve within it | 5 min | 7 min | 7 min | 10 min |
| Lock wait | 20 min | 2 min | 20 min | 2 min |

A `hil-operator` run on the shared rig gets 1 run per firmware per question, plus the contract's retry, and a 20 min lock wait.

A shared rig is one where CI also holds boards (ci.lan); a dev bench has no CI. Size the observation window from the reproducer's latency, scaling wall time with it; budget repetitions for the criterion runs and the auxiliary reproducer invocations (smoke checks, cleanup checks) together; for an intermittent failure, choose repetitions and exposure from the observed failure rate or the issue's requirement. The debugger's finalization budget is separate, from the project's build and check timings and scope. A validator receives at most one capture-method correction within its other ceilings.

Settle a hardware claim from valid observations of the identified firmware under the stated conditions: a reviewer's conclusion or a green build cannot overturn a reproduced failure, and a passing run cannot refute an unexercised scenario. Only `hw-validator` returns `fixed`. `not-reproduced` neither fixes nor refutes an earlier reproduction, `inconclusive` is never `rig-side`, and neither turns a red check green. Reuse a result only while its firmware, configuration, reproducer and rig inputs are unchanged.

Relaunch `hw-debugger` while its last round's `changed` is non-empty and evidenced, its cleanup succeeded, the scope still holds and the budget fits the named next experiment; the new dispatch hands over that round's hypotheses, patches, reusable build artifacts and next experiment. Accept a `changed` entry only when its cited run or artifact establishes a valid first reproduction; a localized first failure that narrows the failing mechanism; a causal hypothesis supported, refuted or distinguished by a discriminating observation, including an unexpected behavioural difference that does so; an observation defect repaired so a needed experiment is now possible; or a candidate's predicted behavioural difference demonstrated, and names the uncertainty removed. More logs, another technique, a build, a repeated observation or repeating the same measurement repair do not qualify. Continued diagnosis needs a named next distinguishing experiment; a completed diagnosis goes to finalization or validation and needs none. An empty `changed` stops diagnosis: `needs-user` when continuing needs a human decision, information or authorization, otherwise report the technical blocker or the completed inconclusive investigation. A pending finalization continues when its blocker is resolved or an adequate budget for the remaining work is supplied; dispatch it explicitly as finalization-only, carrying the expected checkout state, candidate patch if any, prior evidence and cleanup receipt, fix scope, implementation and commit scope and finalization budget. It does not acquire or touch hardware. The same finalization-only dispatch carries verified review findings on a committed supported fix, with the reviewed commit and the findings, when the assigned fix scope covers the follow-up; a scope gap returns to you or the launcher, not automatically to a human. A finding that changes the supported cause or needs a new experiment goes back to diagnosis. The follow-up commit is a new HEAD: commit check, a fresh `hw-validator` and review again. There is no round cap. A committed fix goes to a fresh `hw-validator` with the same criterion and the debugger's reproducer; a valid reproduction of the original failure on that commit returns to `hw-debugger` with the commit and that failure as its handoff, while a rig failure, an invalid capture or an unchanged inconclusive result does not by itself justify another round.

Dispatch a hardware unit only after active writers, reviews and pr-babysit have terminated and any hardware unit or HIL run you launched in the same checkout or on its boards or probe has finished, a run the repository's HIL contract reports as holding its board for recovery counting as finished for that recovery's dispatch; from dispatch through its checked cleanup, launch none of them. After every hardware unit check yourself the expected branch and HEAD, clean status and the history since the pre-dispatch SHA: no commits from `hw-validator` or from a debugger round that did not commit, only the fix paths from one that did, with its build and hook evidence. Schedule further use of the board only after that check passes and the cleanup receipt confirms all applicable cleanup succeeded. A failed restoration or a dead unit gets a recovery dispatch of the same role owning its recorded paths and hardware state. For a hardware bug the order is the facts check, `hw-debugger` rounds to a committed fix (for an issue: diagnosis, checked cleanup, the co-planned finalization, as Fix issue N says), checked cleanup and the commit check, the due simplification challenge with any resulting writer batch and commit check, then a fresh `hw-validator` establishing the acceptance criterion on the resulting committed pristine HEAD, artifact cleanup and review of the production diff; a simplification hypothesis requiring hardware investigation returns to `hw-debugger` before that validation, and relevant HIL results may supply evidence to it. Until the validator flashes the fix, the bench holds the pinned restoration firmware or the contract's cleanup state, either of which may predate the fix. Artifact cleanup removes owned generated artifacts from the checkout and keeps the evidence and handoff artifacts that unit results reference, reporting where they are. Inside a PR that pr-babysit drives, the repair then rejoins it through `adoptHead`, as the pr-babysit paragraph says.
